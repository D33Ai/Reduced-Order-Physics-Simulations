SOURCE — NASA PDS Geosciences, LRO LOLA GDR polar DEM ldem_875s_5m (5 m/px, polar stereographic, south pole). 25.6 km window centred on the pole, resampled to 512² @ 50 m. Elevation relative to a 1737.4 km sphere.
DERIVED (F2) — mean illumination by ray-marching the real DEM over a 24-azimuth sweep at 1.5° solar elevation; the rendered shadows use a second, directional visibility raster swept at the actual sun azimuth with a 0.53° solar-disc penumbra. Surface temperature by radiative equilibrium (Stefan–Boltzmann) on the real slope; PSRs by connected components; landing pad by slope/illumination screening; rover route by A* with an 18° grade limit.
MODELLED (F2) — the descent is flown end to end: DOI from a 100 km circular orbit by vis-viva, coast to a 15.2 km periapsis, then powered descent — braking, approach and terminal phases — under ZEM/ZEV guidance closing on the navigated state. Planar point-mass translation carries the curvature terms (vx²/r and 1/r² gravity); second-order pitch attitude with real inertia, RCS torque limit and a braking-aware rate law; throttle first-order lag and deep-throttle floor; radar altimeter noise/lag, IMU drift, and a downrange position error that shrinks when the base beacon is acquired. Touchdown is on the real terrain height under the vehicle.
SYNTHETIC (F1, cosmetic) — sub-50 m surface roughness and the small-crater field (below DEM post spacing); all terrain beyond the 25.6 km LOLA window, which exists only to give the approach and the horizon somewhere to be; plume, ejecta, engine-scoured ground, base and vehicle geometry. None of this reaches any screening number — site slope and undulation are measured on the DEM alone.
NOT CLAIMED — this is not CFD, not validated flight GNC, not a certified design tool, and not an ice detection. Cold traps are temperature-stability candidates derived from topography alone — no neutron, radar or UV data. Vehicle mass, thrust, inertia and gear limits are Apollo-LM-class assumptions, exposed in the source so they can be changed.
